On taking the slower road.

Why passing tests, writing elegant explanations and making real progress are not quite the same thing.

It is possible to build something that looks ready before it can be trusted. The interface works. The examples are tidy. The explanation sounds convincing. None of that guarantees the science is right.

Ontosma is an experimental project, and we have already learned to separate different kinds of progress. An engineering test can show that a tool returns a record correctly. It cannot show that an AI system interpreted the experiment well or chose the right follow-up question.

There are several ways to be wrong.

A source can be genuine while the claim it is used for is unsupported. A calculation can be reproducible while the statistical design is inappropriate. An assistant can remember a hypothesis perfectly and still fail to challenge it when it should.

These are not reasons to avoid building. They are reasons to be careful about what we celebrate.

Reliability is the starting point. Scientific insight is the harder test.

We should be willing to find no advantage.

There are already strong research agents and general-purpose AI assistants. Ontosma has to show a difference in the quality of scientific work, not just in the number of tools it can call or the length of its answers.

That means testing unfamiliar questions, looking closely at negative results, and checking whether Ontosma changes a researcher’s decision for good reasons. It also means accepting that a simpler tool may be better on some tasks.

Why share the work now?

Because the questions are worth making public even before the answers are finished. This site is a small record of the thinking behind Ontosma, not a promise that the research has already succeeded.

If there is eventually a useful product here, it should be able to explain not only what it can do, but where its confidence ends.
