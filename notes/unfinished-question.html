The question we couldn’t finish.

A small development story about the distance between finding evidence and finishing an investigation.

From the Ontosma notebook
Vol. 01 / No. 05
← All research notes

Not every useful research session ends with an answer. One of our development investigations began with a biological question about ageing in yeast. The system located and read two relevant primary papers. It did not finish the investigation.

That is the result of this particular attempt. We cannot turn it into a successful analysis, and we have no new biological conclusion to report from it.

Reading is not the same as investigating.

Finding the right literature matters. But a researcher still has to work out what the evidence means for the question at hand, whether there is relevant data to examine, and what conclusion can actually be supported. In this attempt, the work stopped before that point.

A polished summary of the two papers might have looked convincing. It would also have obscured the important fact that the scientific question remained unanswered.

An unfinished investigation should look unfinished.

What we took from it

It reinforced a distinction we care about in building Ontosma: a system can perform useful intermediate steps without completing a useful scientific investigation. We want to evaluate the whole journey, including what researchers can genuinely use at the end.

That means keeping a place in our records for attempts that stall, questions that remain open, and conclusions we decide not to make. There is no value in dressing those up as discoveries.

What comes next

Our next tests need to ask whether a fresh research question can reach a bounded, well-supported answer, with the evidence and uncertainty made clear. We will share selected lessons when they are checked and appropriate to release. The implementation details and unfinished experiments remain private.
