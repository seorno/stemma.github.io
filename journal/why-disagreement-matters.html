Why disagreement in biology should stay visible.

A conflicting dataset is not automatically a bad dataset. Sometimes the disagreement is the biological signal telling you that context matters.

Stemma · October 2026 · 6 min read

Biology rarely gives the same answer under every condition. A cell type can behave differently across developmental stage, culture system, perturbation, species, tissue environment or measurement platform. Yet analysis often pushes us toward one compressed conclusion.

That compression is useful when the evidence truly converges. It becomes dangerous when disagreement is treated as noise simply because it is inconvenient.

Conflict can be information.

Suppose one dataset supports a proposed cell state while another does not. There are several possibilities: the original claim may be weak, the second experiment may differ in a biologically important way, the state may be transient, or the measurement may simply be insufficient to judge. Those possibilities are not equivalent.

The useful question is not only “which dataset is right?” but “what would have to be true for both observations to make sense?”

That shift matters because it turns contradiction into a hypothesis generator. Instead of averaging away the difference, a researcher can ask which context, marker program, perturbation or timing variable explains it.

Unknown deserves its own category.

Another common failure is to treat missing evidence as evidence against a claim. If the relevant state, gene program or condition was not measured with sufficient coverage, the correct answer may simply be that the claim cannot yet be judged in that context.

Keeping “support”, “contradiction” and “unknown” separate sounds simple. In practice, it changes how teams discuss evidence because it prevents confidence from being manufactured by absence.

Good scientific software should preserve this.

The goal of Stemma is not to force biology into a cleaner story. It is to make the structure of the evidence easier to inspect: where independent observations converge, where they conflict, what assumptions sit underneath the interpretation, and where the evidence is still missing.

A useful system should make the next scientific question sharper. If disagreement disappears inside the software, that opportunity disappears with it.
